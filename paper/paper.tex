\begin{abstract}
  In an exploration of building simplicial and cubical set models in indexed form, we extend a previous construction of semi-simplicial and semi-cubical sets with degeneracies. The degeneracies we construct can be characterized as adding a new dimension, and coincide with usual degeneracies in the cubical set case. As necessitated by the construction, we refined the degeneracies and coherence conditions outlined in Grandis-Mauri~\cite{grandis03} to make it step-wise. Our work includes a mechanically checked formalization in Rocq.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Motivation}
Semi-simplicial sets are usually defined as presheaves from the semi-simplicial category, termed as the \emph{fibered} form~\cite{?}. In our previous work, we proposed a uniform construction of semi-simplicial and semi-cubical sets using iterated parametericity, termed as the \emph{indexed} form~\cite{hr25}, using the \emph{fibred-indexed correspondence}. It was originally suggested at Univalent Foundations IAS 2012~\cite{UFwiki2012} that the open problem of constructing semi-simplicial \emph{types} could be solved with the indexed construction, as it supplies more definitional equalities for tackling the combinatorial problem of solving coherence conditions~\footnote{It was originally postulated that the coherences would come for free with the indexed construction, but this turns out not to be the case.}.

\subsection{Contributions}
We previously exploited the observation that semi-simplicial and semi-cubical sets are naturally unary and binary cases of iterated parametricity~\cite{hr25}. In adding a new dimension to our construction, we observed the usual cubical degeneracies in the cubical case and \emph{connections}~\cite{bernardy15,bernardy12} in the simplicial case. The coherence conditions we used to complete our construction made explicit the ones outlined in Grandis-Mauri~\cite{grandis03}. Our work includes a mechanically-checked formalization in Rocq~\href{https://github.com/artagnon/bonak}{\linkicon}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\nu$}{nu}-shape sets}

The semi-simplicial and semi-cubical categories are instances of the more general notion of $\nu$-semi-shape category.

\begin{definition}[$\hexagon$]
  The definition of $\nu$-semi-shape category is shown below. Note that, if $g \circ f$ is well-defined, then the length of $f$ is less than or equal to that of $g$. It can be shown that composition is associative and that $\id$ is neutral.
  \begin{align*}
    \Obj_{\hexagon}       & := \Nat                                                                          \\
    \Hom_{\hexagon}(p, n) & := \{l \in (\nu \sqcup \{\kstar\})^n \mid \text{number of $\kstar$ in $l = p$}\} \\
    g \circ f             & :=
    \begin{cases}
      f                & \text{if $g = \epsilon$}                                                   \\
      a\,(g' \circ f)  & \text{if $g = a\,g'$}, \text{where $a \in \nu$}                            \\
      a\,(g' \circ f') & \text{if $g = \kstar\,g'$, $f = a\,f'$, where $a \in \nu$ or $a = \kstar$} \\
    \end{cases}          \\
    \id                   & := \kstar \ldots \kstar \text{ $n$ times for $\id \in \Hom_{\hexagon}(n, n)$}
  \end{align*}
\end{definition}

In particular $1$-semi-shape category presents the semi-simplicial category while the $2$-semi-shape category presents the semi-cubical category (note that this also relates to unary and binary parametricity~\cite{bernardy15, reynolds83}).

Degeneracies can be added to the $\nu$-semi-shape category in different ways. We here focus on cubical-style degeneracies as in Grandis~\cite{grandis03} and their adaptation to the unary case as in \cite{bernardy15}. A morphism from $p$ to $n$ in the $\nu$-shape category is the composition of a $q$-long degeneracy from $p$ to $p-q$ followed by a face map from $p-q$ to $n$.

\begin{definition}[$\hexagon_d$]
  The definition of $\nu$-shape category extends the $\nu$-shape category with degeneracies:
  \begin{align*}
    \Obj_{\hexagon_d}       & := \Nat                                                                                                                           \\
    \Hom_{\hexagon_d}(p, n) & := \{(q,r,l) \mid r \in \{\kstar,\bullet\}^q \wedge l \in (\nu \sqcup \{\kstar\})^n \wedge \text{number of $\kstar$ in $l = q$}\} \\
    (0,g) \circ (q,f)       & := \left(q,
    \begin{cases}
      f                & \text{if $g = \epsilon$}                                                   \\
      a\,(g' \circ f)  & \text{if $g = a\,g'$}, \text{where $a \in \nu$}                            \\
      a\,(g' \circ f') & \text{if $g = \kstar\,g'$, $f = a\,f'$, where $a \in \nu$ or $a = \kstar$} \\
    \end{cases}\right)                                                             \\
    (r+1,g) \circ (q,f)     & :=
    \begin{cases}
      (q+r+1,g)        & \text{if $f = \epsilon$}                                                   \\
      a\,(g' \circ f)  & \text{if $g = a\,g'$}, \text{where $a \in \nu$}                            \\
      a\,(g' \circ f') & \text{if $g = \kstar\,g'$, $f = a\,f'$, where $a \in \nu$ or $a = \kstar$} \\
    \end{cases}                                                             \\
    \\
    \id                     & := \kstar \ldots \kstar \text{ $n$ times for $\id \in \Hom_{\hexagon_d}(n, n)$}
  \end{align*}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Fibred-indexed correspondence}

Semi-cubical sets can be expressed directly in terms of presheaves over a direct category using morphisms or alternatively using families, by iterating the standard equivalence between maps and families:
$(\Sigma A : Set. A \rightarrow B) \equiv (B \rightarrow Set)$.

\begin{equation*}
  \begin{array}{cllccllll}
    \multicolumn{3}{c}{\mbox{\emph{fibred form}}} & \quad\mathit{vs}\quad & \multicolumn{3}{l}{\mbox{\emph{indexed form}}}                                                                                                                                                                                                     \\
    \\
    Y_0                                           & :                     & \HSet[l]                                       &  & X_0                                                                                & :                & \HSet[l]                                                           & \mbox{(points)}   \\
    \uparrow\uparrow                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           \\
    Y_1                                           & :                     & \HSet[l]                                       &  & X_1                                                                                & :                & X_0 \times X_0 \rightarrow \HSet[l]                                & \mbox{(segments)} \\
    \uparrow\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow              &                       &                                                &  &                                                                                                                                                                                                \\
    Y_2                                           & :                     & \HSet[l]                                       &  & X_2                                                                                & :                & \Pi (x^0_{LL},x^0_{LR}).\, \Pi x^1_{L*}:X_1 (x^0_{LL},x^0_{LR}).                       \\

Both definitions are provably equivalent using univalence~\href{https://github.com/artagnon/bonak/tree/master/theories/Equiv}{\linkicon}, and this is yet another instance of the \emph{Grothendieck construction}, which we refer to as the fibred-indexed correspondence.

In this paper, we focus on an indexed definition of simplicial sets equipped with faces and a \emph{cubical-like} form of degeneracy and on cubical sets with faces and degeneracies. The added degeneracy is \emph{parametric}: in the binary case, it gives a standard cubical degeneracy; in the unary case, it gives a ParamTT-like degeneracy and \emph{not} a simplicial degeneracy.

First, our degeneracy implies a distinguished point $r_{-1}(a)$ for any $a:X_{-1}$. Then:

\begin{equation*}
  \begin{array}{lcccr}
    \begin{tabular}{l}source\\ (over some $a:X_{-1}$)\end{tabular}         & b~~                                                               & \begin{tikzcd}b \arrow[r, dash,"q"] & c\end{tikzcd} \\[1mm]
    \begin{tabular}{l}parametric\\ degeneracy\end{tabular}                 & \begin{tikzcd}b \arrow[r, dash, "r_0(b)"] & r_{-1}(a)\end{tikzcd} &
    \begin{tikzcd}
      & |[alias=F]|r_{-1}(a) \arrow[ddr, dash, "r_0(c)"] & \\\\
      b \arrow[rr, dash, "q"{name=T, below}]\arrow[uur, dash, "r_0(b)"] && c \\
      \arrow[rightarrow, from=F, to=T, phantom, "r_1(q)" description]
    \end{tikzcd}                                                                                                                         \\[-8mm]
    \begin{tabular}{l}simplicial\\ degeneracy\end{tabular}                 & \begin{tikzcd}b \arrow[r, dash, "s_0(b)"] & b\end{tikzcd}         &
    \begin{tikzcd}
      & |[alias=F]|c \arrow[ddr, dash, "s_0(c)"] & \\\\
      b \arrow[rr, dash, "q"{name=T, below}]\arrow[uur, dash, "q"] && c \\
      \arrow[rightarrow, from=F, to=T, phantom, "s_1(q)" description]
    \end{tikzcd} & \begin{tabular}{l}actually\\ a 1-connection!\end{tabular}
  \end{array}

For any $(X_0,X_1,\ldots): \X$, we define a stream of degeneracies:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{array}{lll}
    \Xrefl(X_0,X_1,\ldots) \defeq                                                                                             \\
    \quad \Sigma r_{0}:\Pi d:\fullframe[0].\Pi x:X_0(d).X_1(\reflff[0][d=\d],\lambda \epsilon.x).                             \\
    \quad \Sigma r_1:\Pi d:\fullframe[1][D={X_0}].\,\Pi x:X_1(d).X_2(\reflff[1][D={r_0}, d=\d],\lambda \epsilon.x).           \\
    \quad \Sigma r_2:\Pi d:\fullframe[2][D={X_0,X_1}].\,\Pi x:X_2(d).X_3(\reflff[2][D={r_0,r_1}, d=\d],\lambda \epsilon.\,x). \\
    \quad \ldots
  \end{array}
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
  \reflff[n][D={r_{-1},\ldots,r_{n-1}}]:\fullframe[n][D={X_{-1},\ldots,X_{n-1}}] \rightarrow \framep[n+1][n][][D={X_{-1},\ldots,X_{n}}]
\end{equation*}
computes the $n$ first layers of the border of $r_{n}(d)(x)$, knowing that the last layer is made of $\nu$ times $x$ itself, so that

\begin{equation*}
  (\reflff[n][D={r_{-1},\ldots,r_{n-1}},d=\d],\lambda \epsilon.\,x): \framep[n+1][n+1][][D={X_{-1},\ldots,X_{n}}]
\end{equation*}
is a full frame.

On the way, we need two coherence conditions:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{array}{l}
    \mathsf{\textcolor{chestnut}{idrestrrefl}}_{\textcolor{indian-yellow}{\mathsf{frame}},\epsilon}^n(r_{-1},\ldots,r_{n-1})(d:\fullframe[n][D={X_0,\ldots,X_{n-1}}]):        \\
    ~~ \restrf[n][n][][][][d={\reflff[n][D={r_{-1},\ldots,r_{n-1}},d=\d]}] = d                                                                                                \\
    \\
